**How a date becomes a topic.** Every topic is assigned a category at Plan mode, alongside its format.

## THE TOPIC GENERATION CHAIN

```
1. Current date
        ↓
2. Festival calendar — what is upcoming inside 14 days?
   (verified rows only; never calculate a date)
        ↓
3. Trend scan — what is being searched and discussed
   around that occasion RIGHT NOW?
        ↓
4. Assign a CATEGORY
        ↓
5. Assign a FORMAT
        ↓
6. Write the hook
```

**The calendar supplies the occasion. The trend scan supplies the angle. The category supplies the shape.**

If nothing falls inside 14 days, the chain starts from `recurring-observances.md` instead — there are always at least 9 anchor dates in any month.

# CATEGORY 1 · MYTHOLOGICAL CONNECTION

**Take the upcoming occasion, find the scripture story behind it, and reveal the part nobody tells.**

Reference: Sri Mandir's Pitru Paksha reel connects shradh to Karna — a man who gave gold generously his whole life but never offered anna and jal to his own ancestors, and what happened to him because of it.

### Shape

```
Upcoming date  →  the practice it involves
               →  WHICH TEXT explains that practice
               →  the story in that text
               →  the story contains a surprise
               →  bridge back to the viewer today
```

### Source texts by occasion

| Occasion | Primary text | Story seam |
|---|---|---|
| Pitru Paksha, Shradh, Tarpan | **Garuda Purana**, Mahabharata | Karna's debt; the fate of unoffered ancestors |
| Pind Daan at Gaya | Ramayana, local Gaya mahatmya | Sita and the four false witnesses |
| Navratri, Durga Puja | **Markandeya Purana** (Devi Mahatmya) | Mahishasura's boon; why a goddess was needed |
| Sharad Navratri specifically | Ramayana | Akaal Bodhan — Ram invoking Durga out of season |
| Diwali, Lakshmi Puja | Ramayana, **Bhagavata Purana** | Narakasura; the return to Ayodhya |
| Ganesh Chaturthi | **Shiva Purana** | The broken tusk; the moon's curse |
| Ekadashi (each of 24) | **Padma Purana** | Every Ekadashi has its own mahatmya story |
| Tulsi Vivah | Padma Purana | Vrinda and Jalandhar |
| Satyanarayan Puja | **Skanda Purana** (Reva Khanda) | The merchant who broke his vow |
| Maha Shivaratri | Shiva Purana | The night Shiva drank the poison |
| Karwa Chauth | Mahabharata, folk Veervati | Draupadi's vrat |
| Govardhan Puja | Bhagavata Purana | Krishna lifting the hill |
| Raksha Bandhan | Mahabharata | Krishna and Draupadi's cloth |

### Format

**Always Format B (Narrative Story).** Hook formula: `origin-reveal`.

### Hard rules

- **Name the text.** "गरुड़ पुराण के अनुसार", "भागवत पुराण में कहा गया है" — the viewer should know where it comes from.
- **Hedge every claim.** The market leader attributes four times in a 65-second script: प्रचलित कथा के अनुसार · लोक मान्यता में · मान्यता है कि · माना जाता है. Do the same.
- **Never invent a verse, a chapter number or a quotation.** If a specific citation is wanted, it is a `[VERIFY]` slot the pandit fills.
- **Never invent a story detail** to make it better. If the story is thin, pick a different one.
- **Regional variants exist.** When a story is told differently in different traditions, say so — "कुछ कथाओं में…" is a stronger reel than pretending there is one version.
- **The pandit verifies two things:** that the story exists in the named text, and that it is told correctly.

### Why this works

The audience already performs the practice. They have never been told where it comes from. That gap is the hook, and the scripture is the payload — which also makes it the most defensible content you can make, because you are reporting tradition rather than asserting doctrine.

# CATEGORY 2 · SCIENTIFIC CONNECTION

**Take a traditional practice and place it beside what modern research actually says.**

This category has the highest engagement potential and the highest credibility risk in the whole library. It is governed by a hard evidence rule.

### ⚠️ THE EVIDENCE TIERS

Every scientific claim is classified before it can be used.

#### TIER 1 — ESTABLISHED · usable

The mechanism is documented in mainstream science, independent of the ritual.

- **Namaskar without physical contact** reduces contact-borne transmission
- **Morning sunlight exposure** affects circadian rhythm and vitamin D synthesis
- **Bathing before worship** — basic hygiene
- **Communal gathering and ritual** — documented effects on social cohesion and mental wellbeing
- **Slow, extended exhalation** (as in chanting) affects heart-rate variability and the parasympathetic response

#### TIER 2 — RESEARCH EXISTS ON THE BEHAVIOUR, NOT THE RITUAL CLAIM · usable with careful wording

There is real research on the underlying activity. There is **no** research showing the ritual was designed for it.

- **Fasting** (Ekadashi, vrat) — intermittent fasting research exists; it is not research on Ekadashi
- **Tulsi, neem, turmeric** — documented pharmacology; not evidence that ritual use was medicinal
- **Sitting cross-legged on the floor** — posture and mobility research
- **Camphor and dhoop** — some antimicrobial evidence, frequently overstated

**Required wording:** *"उपवास पर आधुनिक शोध क्या कहता है"* — what modern research says about fasting. **Never** *"हमारे ऋषियों को ये पहले से पता था"* — our sages already knew this.

#### TIER 3 — NOT SUPPORTED · BANNED

Never use. Not as a hedge, not as "some believe", not at all.

- ❌ "Positive energy" / "negative energy" presented as measurable
- ❌ "Divine energy", "cosmic rays", "vibrations" as physics
- ❌ "Science has proven chakras are activated"
- ❌ "Sun rays through falling water enter the body and give energy"
- ❌ "NASA has confirmed…" (almost always false)
- ❌ Quantum physics invoked for anything spiritual
- ❌ Any claim that a ritual cures, prevents or treats a disease

The example image circulating as "Science of Namaskar" — labelling arrows as *divine energy*, *Chaitanya*, *positive energy produced by the action* — is **Tier 3 throughout.** It is the version of this category to avoid.

### The honest framing template

```
परंपरा  →  what the practice is, stated respectfully
शोध     →  what research says about the UNDERLYING BEHAVIOUR
संतुलन  →  both stand on their own
```

Worked example — **Surya Arghya**:

| | |
|---|---|
| ✅ Usable | The practice means standing in early sunlight for a few minutes each morning. Research on morning light exposure and circadian rhythm is well established. |
| ❌ Banned | Sun rays passing through the falling water enter your eyes and give you positive energy. |

Same topic. One is interesting and true. The other is checkable and false.

### The rule that protects you from both sides

> **Never imply the practice needs science to be valid.**

Science is an interesting parallel, not a justification. A reel that says "see, it was scientific all along" invites two attacks at once: rationalists who check the claim, and traditionalists who ask why dharma needs Western validation.

The safe position: *"ये परंपरा सदियों से चली आ रही है। दिलचस्प बात ये है कि आधुनिक शोध भी इस आदत के बारे में कुछ कहता है।"*

### Format

**Format D (Narrated Explainer)** usually — abstract visuals, no presenter, no ritual close-ups.
**Format A** when a presenter delivering it adds authority.

Hook formula: `practical-doubt` or a new `tradition-and-research` angle.

### Double verification gate

This category needs **two** sign-offs, not one:

| Gate | Who | Checks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pandit | Is the practice described correctly? Is the tradition stated accurately? |
| 2 | **Anyone with a science background** | Is the research claim Tier 1 or Tier 2? Is the wording free of Tier 3 language? |

**If gate 2 cannot be staffed, do not run this category.** A wrong scripture reference is forgivable. A debunked science claim screenshot circulates forever.

## CATEGORY MIX

Per month, 8 reels:

| Category | Count | Typical format |
|---|---|---|
| Mythological connection | 2 | B |
| Scientific connection | 1–2 | D |
| Practical doubt / explainer | 2 | D or A |
| Service, FAQ, observable behaviour | 2 | A |
| Promo | 1 | A |

**Mythological is the safest and most shareable.** Scientific is the highest-risk and should stay at one or two a month until the gate-2 reviewer is reliable.
